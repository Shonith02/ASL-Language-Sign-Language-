ASL Sign Language Chart

Interactive Guide to American Sign Language

American Sign Language (ASL) is a complete, natural language that has the same linguistic properties as spoken languages. ASL is expressed by movements of the hands and face. Explore our comprehensive guide to learn the basics of ASL and improve your signing skills.

A

Closed fist with thumb along the side

B

Open palm with fingers together

C

Curved hand in a C-shape

D

Index finger pointed up, rest curled

E

Fingertips curled into palm

F

Index finger and thumb form circle

1

Closed hand with fingers forming an O

2

Index finger pointing up

3

Index and middle fingers extended

4

First three fingers extended

5

Four fingers extended, thumb tucked

6

All five fingers extended

Hello

Flat hand near forehead, move outward

Thank You

Hand starts at chin, moves forward

Please

Circular motion on chest

Sorry

Fist circular motion over chest

Good

Hand flat to mouth, moves down

Bad

Hand flat to mouth, moves out

Tips for Learning ASL

  • Practice consistently - even 10 minutes daily makes a difference
  • Focus on hand positioning and orientation for accurate signs
  • Don't forget facial expressions - they're a crucial part of ASL grammar
  • Connect with the deaf community to improve your fluency
  • Use our Practice Mode to receive real-time feedback on your signing
